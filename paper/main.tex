\begin{abstract}
Textual-program optimizers use execution traces to revise prompts,
instructions, and agent skills.  Under a fixed rollout budget, candidate
generation and candidate verification draw from the same evidence supply.  The
number of independent proposal--verification attempts determines search
bandwidth, and fresh support for each fixed candidate determines verification
precision.  Adaptive evidence reuse also makes provenance part of the
optimization problem.  This paper formulates \emph{provenance-constrained
evidence routing} with terminal simple regret as its objective.  \method{}
allocates finite search bandwidth and verification support while preserving the
generation--certification boundary across complete lineages.  Recursive
exclusion assigns each candidate a legal evidence set.  Instance-relative credit
compares legally observed candidates on the same instance and updates with the
reward frontier.  A tie-inclusive persistent queue and maximum-weight proposal
allocation distribute search slots across distinct repair opportunities.  Each
fixed child then receives an independently sampled legal admission batch.  Under
the shared budget, more independent attempts reduce the search gap, and more
fresh support increases the precision of candidate selection.
Completed Qwen3-8B held-out owner evaluations span mathematical reasoning
(e.g., AIME-2025), instruction following (IFBench), multi-hop question answering
and fact verification (HotPotQA and HoVer), and privacy-conscious delegation
(PUPA).  Relative to
published GEPA, the largest gain is $25.3$ percentage points on AIME-2025, and
the mean gain across all six shared benchmarks is $7.3$ points.
These results show
that governing how evidence is created, reused, and routed can materially
affect sample-efficient textual-program evolution.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Feedback-driven optimizers revise instructions, prompts, and agent skills from
execution traces.  Reflection, textual gradients, and evolutionary prompt
search use language feedback to produce large behavioral updates with fixed
model weights \citep{reflexion,self_refine,textgrad,
cheng2024traceautodiffgenerativeoptimization,gepa}.  Their common optimization
task is compact: given a seed textual program, an owner evaluator, and rollout
budget $B$, the optimizer adaptively generates and evaluates candidates, then
returns one terminal program.  Every rollout serves one of two statistical
roles.  Proposal evidence diagnoses a failure and shapes a revision.
Verification evidence evaluates a revision whose text is already fixed.  Three
instruction-following cases, for example, may expose a constraint-ordering error
and inspire a new rule.  Fresh owner cases then measure the fixed rule.

Independent proposal--verification attempts set search bandwidth, and fresh
support per fixed candidate sets ranking precision.  Their shared budget trades
broader search against sharper admission and terminal selection.  Lineage
provenance governs the eligible evidence for verification: every batch that
shapes a candidate becomes proposal history for its descendants.  The optimizer
routes this finite budget under a recursive generation--certification boundary.

This paper studies this task as \emph{provenance-constrained evidence routing}.
The routing policy minimizes expected terminal simple regret under a total
rollout budget, finite search bandwidth, finite fresh verification support, and
lineage-level provenance.  A committed partial reward table supplies legal
routing evidence.  The single objective is to return the strongest terminal
program supported by the budget and owner evaluation.

Three challenges govern this objective.  First, birth provenance must propagate
through the full lineage so that each fixed child receives fresh certification
evidence.  Second, a partial and uneven reward table needs a candidate credit
that remains comparable across instance difficulty and updates with new
frontiers.  Third, finite proposal capacity must reach distinct, high-value
repair opportunities across concurrent search paths.

\method{} (\textbf{C}redit-Aware \textbf{O}ptimization with \textbf{M}ini-batch
\textbf{P}roposal and \textbf{A}dmission for \textbf{S}kill \textbf{S}earch)
implements this routing principle through three aligned mechanisms.  Recursive
exclusion propagates the generation--certification boundary and supplies each
fixed child with an owner-sampled legal admission batch.  Instance-relative
credit compares legally observed candidates on the same instance and refreshes
their ordering whenever the reward frontier changes.  The tie-inclusive
Top-$\rho$ envelope and persistent queue preserve candidate coverage, while a
maximum-weight one-to-one allocation sends proposal slots to distinct repair
opportunities.  Figure~\ref{fig:overview} shows how these mechanisms route
limited evidence from candidate generation to terminal selection.

Completed owner evaluations span model families and task types.  On Qwen3-8B,
task-specific COMPASS-family variants reach $57.33\%$ on AIME-2025,
$43.37\%$ on IFBench, $66.67\%$ on LiveBench-Math, and $51.33\%$ on HoVer.
The first three exceed published GEPA by $25.33$, $4.76$, and $14.72$
percentage points; HoVer is $1.00$ point lower.  Each endpoint has an exact
rollout, candidate, token, and cost ledger.  The frozen checkpoints retain
lineage, queue, allocation, and cache-activation state for mechanism analysis.

This paper makes four contributions:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt,topsep=3pt]
  \item \emph{Provenance-constrained evidence routing} casts textual-program
  evolution as terminal simple-regret minimization under explicit constraints on
  rollout budget, search bandwidth, verification support, and lineage
  provenance (\Cref{sec:setting}).

  \item \method{} recursively separates generation from certification and
  turns committed legal evidence into a routing signal based on how each
  candidate compares with its peers on the same instance.  The signal is
  recomputed as the partial observation state changes
  (\Cref{sec:global_credit,sec:lineage_compression}).

  \item A tie-inclusive Top-$\rho$ envelope supplies persistent parent slots,
  and an exact maximum-weight integer assignment couples those slots to
  repairable proposal batches.  Each fixed child receives its own
  provenance-legal owner batch and paired reference gate
  (\Cref{sec:proposal_allocation}).

  \item Frozen COMPASS-family identities are evaluated under fixed published
  budgets across models and tasks, including mathematical reasoning,
  instruction following, and multi-hop fact verification
  (\Cref{sec:experiments}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Provenance-Constrained Evidence Routing}
\label{sec:setting}

\subsection{Budgeted textual-program optimization}

Given a seed textual program $p_0$, an owner evaluator, and rollout budget $B$,
a routing policy $\pi$ adaptively generates and evaluates candidates and returns
a terminal program $p_{\hat s_\pi}$.  Let $R(p)$ denote owner utility and let
$R^\star$ denote the best reachable utility under the search model.  The
budgeted objective is
\[
\pi^\star\in\arg\min_{\pi\in\Pi(B,b_P,b_A)}
\E\!\left[R^\star-R(p_{\hat s_\pi})\right],
\]
where $b_P,b_A\in\mathbb N_+$, with $|P|=b_P$ proposal observations and
$|A|=b_A$ fresh verification observations per attempt.  Policies in
$\Pi(B,b_P,b_A)$ divide their rollout expenditure as
\[
C_{\mathrm{proposal}}(\pi)+C_{\mathrm{verify}}(\pi)\le B.
\]
Proposal expenditure determines the number $M_B(\pi)$ of independently
proposed-and-certified children, which is the search bandwidth.  Verification
expenditure assigns $b_A$ fresh owner observations to each fixed child and
controls the precision of admission and terminal selection.  Larger
$M_B(\pi)$ lowers the search gap, and larger $b_A$ lowers selection uncertainty.

At optimization step $t$, let $\Pcal_t=\{p_0,\ldots,p_{m_t-1}\}$ contain the
accepted textual programs.  Every candidate after $p_0$ records a parent,
inducing a directed acyclic lineage.  The \emph{owner} is the
benchmark-authoritative data, execution, metric, and aggregation contract.
Executing program $p_s$ on optimization instance $x$ yields a bounded reward in
$[0,1]$, an output, and an execution trace.  The committed cache is a partial
table $q_t(s,x)$ containing the best finite owner-accepted observation for each
evaluated pair, with unevaluated pairs represented by missing entries.  Let
$P_a^{\mathrm{birth}}$ be the proposal batch that created candidate $a$, with
$P_0^{\mathrm{birth}}=\varnothing$, and let $\operatorname{Anc}(s)$ contain the
transitive ancestors of $s$.  The lineage birth set is
\[
\mathcal B_s=\bigcup_{a\in\operatorname{Anc}(s)\cup\{s\}}
P_a^{\mathrm{birth}}
\]
and the routing-eligible evidence set
$\Omega_t(s)=\{x:q_t(s,x)\text{ exists and }x\notin\mathcal B_s\}$.
Routing credit for candidate $s$ therefore draws exclusively from observations
outside the batches that shaped its lineage.

The owner supplies the optimization observations accepted into the cache and,
after selection, an independent frozen test.

\subsection{Recursive generation--certification boundary}

For a child proposed from parent $s$ on batch $P$, define
\begin{equation}
X(s,P)=P\cup
\bigcup_{a\in\operatorname{Anc}(s)\cup\{s\}}P_a^{\mathrm{birth}}
\label{eq:recursive_exclusion}
\end{equation}
A legal admission batch satisfies $A\cap X(s,P)=\varnothing$.  Proposal traces
remain available for diagnosis.  Certification for the child and its
descendants uses batches disjoint from current and inherited birth provenance.
In the running example, the three diagnostic cases explain the error, and a
disjoint admission batch decides whether the new rule earns future search
budget.

\subsection{Routing legal evidence}

Let $\mathcal H_t$ contain the lineage, birth provenance, committed cache,
remaining budget, and persistent routing state.  A routing action
  $a_t=(\A_t,\mathbf z_t,Y_t)$ chooses active candidates, persistent parent
  slots, and a one-to-one proposal assignment from $\mathcal H_t$.  The frozen
owner test is reserved for terminal evaluation.  Recursive exclusion supplies
the generation--certification boundary.  Instance-relative credit organizes
uneven partial evidence as frontiers change.  Persistent proposal slots and a
coupled one-to-one assignment coordinate concurrent repair opportunities, and
each fixed child receives an independently sampled admission batch.

A feasible router preserves the recursive boundary, the logical budget $B$,
one-commit cache semantics, and terminal owner separation.  Its optimal policy
minimizes expected simple regret for the returned program.

\section{\method{}: Routing Legal Evidence Through Search}
\label{sec:method}

One \method{} wave carries evidence through a complete lifecycle.  A proposal
batch reveals a failure pattern and shapes a child.  Equation
\eqref{eq:recursive_exclusion} removes the evidence that shaped its lineage
from the child's legal admission pool.  A legal admission batch then judges the
fixed child, eligible outcomes enter the committed cache once, and the new
state determines which lineages and tasks receive the next budget.  The method
therefore couples provenance, reuse, and allocation rather than treating them
as separate heuristics.

\subsection{From provenance to committed evidence}
\label{sec:global_credit}

For parent $s$ and proposal batch $P$, \method{} samples admission instances
only outside $X(s,P)$.  The admission identities, references, and
outcomes remain hidden from the generator until the child text is fixed.
The proposal rollout may update the existing parent, but $P$ becomes the
child's birth provenance and is therefore excluded from that child's
routing-eligible set together with all inherited birth batches.  At birth, the child
enters the committed table only through its legal admission evaluation.
Later owner-accepted executions may enlarge its routing evidence whenever
their instances remain outside the same lineage birth set.  This lets proposal
traces retain their high-bandwidth diagnostic role while admission supplies a
new observation that can certify the revision.

Let $\F_t(x)$ be the owner frontier among candidates for which
$x\in\Omega_t(s)$.  Every current frontier owner counts as a frontier outcome,
irrespective of the frontier's raw score.  Define the legally observed
comparison set and its frontier count by
\[
\mathcal C_t(x)=\{u:x\in\Omega_t(u)\},\qquad
N_x=|\mathcal C_t(x)|,\qquad
y_{sx}=\ind\{s\in\F_t(x)\},\qquad
K_x=\sum_{u\in\mathcal C_t(x)}y_{ux}.
\]
When $N_x\ge2$, candidate $s$ receives the leave-one-skill-out frontier
residual
\[
r_{sx}=y_{sx}-\frac{K_x-y_{sx}}{N_x-1}
=\frac{N_xy_{sx}-K_x}{N_x-1}.
\]
Only comparable exposures enter the aggregate:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\Omega_t^{\mathrm{cmp}}(s)&=\{x\in\Omega_t(s):N_x\ge2\},
&E_s^{\mathrm{cmp}}&=|\Omega_t^{\mathrm{cmp}}(s)|,\\
R_s&=\sum_{x\in\Omega_t^{\mathrm{cmp}}(s)}r_{sx},
&G_s&=\frac{R_s}{E_s^{\mathrm{cmp}}}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:global_key}
\end{equation}
Thus $G_s$ records how often $s$ reaches the current frontier relative to the
other legally observed candidates on the same instances.  An observation with
$N_x=1$ remains in the committed table but contributes no relative credit until
another candidate is legally observed on $x$.  Missing entries remain missing,
and $G_s$ is undefined when $E_s^{\mathrm{cmp}}=0$ rather than being set to
zero.  Whenever a commit changes an observation or an owner frontier, COMPASS
recomputes $N_x$, $K_x$, all affected residuals, and the resulting $G_s$ values.

\subsection{A credit-ranked search frontier}
\label{sec:lineage_compression}

Let $\mathcal R_t=\{s:E_s^{\mathrm{cmp}}>0\}$ be the candidates with defined
credit.  Parent--descendant relations do not remove candidates from this
domain.  Let $\rho\in\mathbb N_+$ be the active-rank cutoff.  If
$|\mathcal R_t|\ge\rho$, let $g_\rho$ be the $\rho$-th largest $G_s$ and retain
every candidate with $G_s\ge g_\rho$; otherwise retain all of
$\mathcal R_t$.  The active set $\A_t$ can therefore contain more than $\rho$
programs at a boundary tie, and a parent and its descendant may remain active
simultaneously.  If no candidate has defined credit, COMPASS falls back to the
seed.

The resulting active set is sorted by decreasing $G_s$, with earlier candidate
index as the deterministic exact-tie order.  Let $w\in\mathbb N_+$ be the wave
width.  A persistent cyclic cursor draws $w$ \emph{slot identities} from this
order.  If $|\A_t|<w$, parent labels repeat but slot identities do not.  The
queue therefore preserves coverage across waves while allowing the current
frontier to concentrate work.

\subsection{Turning credit into proposal work and legal admission}
\label{sec:proposal_allocation}
\label{sec:admission_allocation}
\label{sec:commit}

Candidate credit decides which candidates own the wave's persistent slot
rights; it is not reused as a batch score.  For candidate $s$ and proposal
minibatch $P$, define the
recorded repair opportunity
\begin{equation}
H(s,P)=\frac{1}{|P|}\sum_{x\in P}
\begin{cases}
\left[\max_{r\in\A_t:q_t(r,x)\ \mathrm{finite}}q_t(r,x)-q_t(s,x)\right]_+,
& q_t(s,x)\ \mathrm{finite},\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:repairable_gap}
\end{equation}
The zero branch is an opportunity weight, not an imputed failure.  Let
$\mathcal I_w=\{0,\ldots,w-1\}$ index the drawn slots $z_i$ and proposal
minibatches $P_j$.  \method{} allocates them by the maximum-weight binary
program
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
Y^\star\in\argmax_{Y\in\{0,1\}^{w\times w}}\quad
&\sum_{i,j\in\mathcal I_w}H(z_i,P_j)y_{ij}\\
\text{s.t.}\quad
&\sum_{j\in\mathcal I_w}y_{ij}=1\ \ \forall i,
\qquad
\sum_{i\in\mathcal I_w}y_{ij}=1\ \ \forall j.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:proposal_assignment}
\end{equation}
Thus distinct slots receive distinct proposal rights even when two slots carry
the same parent label, and the wave realizes the largest recorded repair
opportunity available to those slots and batches.

Proposal evaluation can update the existing parent and leaves a set of
surviving proposal tasks.  After these parent observations are committed, write
$m\le w$ for the number of surviving tasks, with parent $s_i$ and proposal
batch $P_i$.  For each task independently, the owner samples an admission batch
$A_i$ satisfying
\[
A_i\cap X(s_i,P_i)=\varnothing.
\]
The owner freezes $A_i$ and, for
each $x\in A_i$, selects a reference from the current frontier owners with
defined $G$, preferring the largest $G$ and using frozen randomness for an
exact tie.  If no such comparable owner exists, the sampled parent $s_i$ is the
reference.  Denote this choice by $r_i(x)$.  Missing reference observations are
evaluated and maximum-bound on their existing programs before the child is
generated, while admission identities, references, and outcomes remain hidden
from the generator.

After the generator fixes child $p_i$ from $(s_i,P_i)$, the owner evaluates the
child and its references on the same batch and applies the paired aggregate
gate
\begin{equation}
\Delta_i=\sum_{x\in A_i}\left[q(p_i,x)-q(r_i(x),x)\right],
\qquad
\operatorname{admit}(p_i)\iff\Delta_i>0.
\label{eq:admission_gate}
\end{equation}
Maximum-bound proposal and admission-reference observations update existing
programs, while only lineage-eligible entries affect their credit; a child's
own admission evidence enters the cache only with an owner-accepted child.
COMPASS then recomputes every affected $G_s$, the tie-inclusive active envelope,
and the queue.  At the budget boundary, the maximal defined $G_s$ is returned,
with earliest index breaking an exact tie; if every $G_s$ is undefined, the
seed is returned.  The terminal
artifact alone enters independent owner evaluation.  Expanded execution and
fallback details appear in \Cref{app:algorithm}.

\section{How \method{} Turns Evidence into Search}
\label{sec:mechanism}

To see how the components fit together, follow one revision through the search
loop.  A proposal batch reveals a parent's failures and shapes a child.  Since
those examples helped create the child, different evidence must establish that
the revision is useful.  The resulting fresh outcomes are compared with other
candidates on the same instances, converted into routing credit, and spent on
the next set of proposal opportunities.  The formal statements below support
three intuitions needed to read this loop: admission tests a fixed revision,
credit records relative distinctions rather than raw successes, and a fixed
rollout budget must trade search breadth against decision precision.

\subsection{A revision is judged on fresh paired evidence}

A revision can look perfect on the examples it was written to solve while
changing nothing elsewhere.  This makes a generation--certification boundary
essential rather than cosmetic.

\begin{proposition}[Self-certification can be arbitrarily optimistic]
\label{prop:self_cert_leakage}
For every admission size $n\ge1$ and $\epsilon>0$, there is a task
distribution and a proposal rule that observes $n$ examples, constructs a
program, and scores one when those examples are reused for certification,
although the program's population utility is at most $\epsilon$.
\end{proposition}

The construction in \Cref{app:proofs} memorizes the observed examples.
COMPASS prevents that evidence from returning as certification for either the
child that used it or later descendants that can inherit the resulting
behavior.

\paragraph{Fresh-pair principle.}
The proposal batch explains how to change the parent; a disjoint admission
batch decides whether that change helps.  Before generation, the owner freezes
the legal admission batch and its per-instance references and hides both from
the generator.  The gate in \Cref{eq:admission_gate} then compares the fixed
child and its references on the same fresh instances.  With $n$ bounded pairs,
the uncertainty of this paired advantage shrinks proportionally to
$1/\sqrt n$.  Thus additional admission evidence makes the decision less
sensitive to a lucky minibatch.  The exact non-anticipation statement and
concentration bound appear in \Cref{thm:recursive_fresh}.

\subsection{Fresh outcomes become instance-relative credit}

Admission decides whether a particular child enters the pool.  Routing asks a
different question: which accepted candidates have shown distinctions worth
pursuing further?  COMPASS answers it by comparing candidates on the same
instance before aggregating across the partial cache.

\begin{proposition}[Instance-wise credit conservation]
\label{prop:credit_conservation}
For every instance $x$ with $N_x\ge2$, the residuals defined immediately
before \Cref{eq:global_key} satisfy
\[
\sum_{s\in\mathcal C_t(x)}r_{sx}=0.
\]
\end{proposition}

This conservation law makes the score a contrast rather than a count.  If all
$N_x$ compared candidates share the current frontier, every residual is zero:
an instance that separates nobody raises nobody's routing priority.  If one
candidate uniquely owns the frontier, it receives residual one and each of the
other $N_x-1$ candidates receives $-1/(N_x-1)$.  Thus the same frontier event
means more when it distinguishes a candidate from its observed peers.

These residuals are not immutable rewards.  When a new committed observation
changes $\mathcal F_t(x)$, COMPASS recomputes $K_x$, every affected $r_{sx}$,
and the corresponding $G_s$.  An instance with only one legal observation
remains cached but begins contributing relative credit only after another
candidate becomes comparable there.

Ancestry imposes no separate survival mask; it determines which observations a
candidate may claim.  Once $G_s$ is defined, the tie-inclusive Top-$\rho$ rule
ranks every candidate in one pool, so a parent and its descendant can remain
active together.  A persistent cursor then converts this credit order into
proposal-slot rights without repeatedly resetting attention to the first
candidate.

\subsection{Credit directs the next wave}

Three signals play different roles in the next wave.  First, $G_s$ determines
which candidates receive the $w$ persistent slots.  Second, the recorded
repair opportunity $H(s,P)$ chooses which distinct proposal batch each slot
receives through the exact binary assignment in
\Cref{eq:proposal_assignment}.  Credit therefore chooses \emph{whose} search
continues, while $H$ chooses \emph{where} each proposal attempt is spent.

Admission is not a second allocation problem.  Each surviving child receives
its own independently sampled legal batch and frozen references, and the
paired gate in \Cref{eq:admission_gate} decides whether it joins the pool.
Accepted evidence can change the frontiers on its instances, which redistributes
the corresponding residuals and updates the next credit order.  The resulting
feedback loop is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\text{fresh outcomes}
&\longrightarrow G_s
\longrightarrow \text{proposal-slot rights}\\
&\longrightarrow H\text{-matched proposals}
\longrightarrow \text{fresh paired admission}.
\end{aligned}
\]
At the budget boundary, the maximal defined $G_s$ is returned, with the seed
used only when every credit remains undefined.  The appendix records cyclic
slot coverage and exact optimality of the proposal assignment.

\subsection{Why breadth and confidence compete}

Let $\Pcal_B$ be the candidates discovered within budget $B$ and let
$\hat p_B$ be COMPASS's actual $G_s$-selected terminal program.  Its regret
has the exact decomposition
\begin{equation}
R^\star-R(\hat p_B)
=
\underbrace{R^\star-\max_{p\in\Pcal_B}R(p)}_{\text{search gap}}
+
\underbrace{\max_{p\in\Pcal_B}R(p)-R(\hat p_B)}_{\text{selection gap}}.
\label{eq:terminal_regret_decomposition}
\end{equation}
The first term falls only when search discovers stronger candidates; the
second falls only when the available evidence identifies a strong discovered
candidate.  A fixed rollout budget must support both.

The following calculation exposes the design law behind this
breadth--confidence tradeoff.

\begin{proposition}[Fresh-evidence breadth--confidence tradeoff]
\label{prop:bandwidth_precision}
For any $M,n\in\mathbb N_+$ and $\delta\in(0,1)$, let
$p_1,\ldots,p_M$ be adaptively generated candidates.  After $p_i$ is fixed,
suppose it receives $n$ conditionally iid owner rewards in $[0,1]$ from one
common owner law with mean $R(p_i)$.  Let $\widehat R_n(p_i)$ be their mean and
define
\[
\widetilde p_{M,n}\in\argmax_{i\le M}\widehat R_n(p_i).
\]
Then, with probability at least $1-\delta$,
\begin{equation}
R^\star-R(\widetilde p_{M,n})
\le
\underbrace{R^\star-\max_{i\le M}R(p_i)}_{\text{search gap}}
+
\underbrace{2\sqrt{\frac{\log(2M/\delta)}{2n}}}_{\text{selection noise}}.
\label{eq:bandwidth_variance_regret}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

The bound yields a direct design principle: more distinct proposal attempts can
reduce the search gap, while more fresh support for each candidate shrinks the
uncertainty of choosing among them.  COMPASS addresses both demands explicitly:
credit-ranked slots and exact proposal matching spend budget on search breadth,
while provenance-legal paired admission spends evidence on each decision.  The
resulting $G_s$ order directs both continued search and terminal selection.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Evaluation questions}

We evaluate whether provenance-constrained routing improves held-out owner
utility under a fixed budget and whether the result persists across models and
tasks.

\subsection{Tasks, models, and owner evaluation}

Our study spans AIME-2025 and LiveBench-Math mathematical reasoning, IFBench
instruction following with unseen constraint types, HotPotQA question
answering, HoVer multi-hop fact verification, PUPA privacy-conscious
delegation

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Reflective textual-program optimization.}
Reflexion and Self-Refine use language feedback to revise behavior, while
TextGrad, Trace/OptoPrime, MIPROv2, and GEPA optimize textual components from
execution evidence
\citep{reflexion,self_refine,textgrad,
cheng2024traceautodiffgenerativeoptimization,miprov2,gepa}.  COMPASS focuses on
evidence routing: which observations may certify a lineage, how a candidate's
frontier contribution should be measured against other legally observed
candidates on the same instance, and where the next proposal budget should
flow.

\paragraph{Evolution, illumination, and skill search.}
Evolutionary prompt and skill methods maintain multiple candidates to avoid a
single greedy trajectory~\citep{gepa,skillpro,grasp,evoskills,metaskillevolve}.
COMPASS differs by keeping parents and descendants in one credit-ranked
candidate pool and using a tie-inclusive Top-$\rho$ envelope rather than an
ancestry-based handoff.

\paragraph{Adaptive evaluation and budget allocation.}
TRIPLE, POES, and Bayesian-Agent allocate evaluation or retrieval effort under
limited budgets~\citep{triple,poes,bayesianagent}; TextBO and APEX optimize
evaluation efficiency and development-example choice
\citep{kang2026textbo,wang2026apex}.  COMPASS couples instance-relative
candidate credit, exact one-to-one proposal work, and independently sampled
legality-first admission in one committed state.  Its credit is a
within-instance frontier residual rather than a raw aggregate reward or
frontier frequency.

\paragraph{Generalization after adaptive search.}
Repeated adaptive use of optimization data can widen the gap between selected
validation performance and fresh test performance
\citep{dwork2015generalization}.  GEPA addresses this empirically through
reusable rules and branch diversity~\citep{gepa}.  COMPASS adds recursive
certification separation and an instance-relative frontier residual that is
recomputed as the committed comparison state changes.  Fixed owner tests
measure the resulting endpoint.

\section{Conclusion}

We have treated textual-program evolution as a fixed-budget information
problem.  Fresh support reduces decision noise, independent candidates increase
search bandwidth, and provenance determines whether reused evidence may certify
a lineage.  COMPASS operationalizes this tradeoff through recursive exclusion,
dynamically recomputed instance-relative credit, a tie-inclusive candidate
envelope, exact proposal assignment, and direct legal admission.  Completed
Qwen3-8B owner evaluations span IFBench, AIME-2025, HoVer, and LiveBench-Math;
the GPT-5.4-nano matrix spans all six shared tasks.  COMPASS contributes the
routing state rather than a new text generator.  This state determines which
evidence creates credit and which proposal work that credit may purchase.

\section{Notation and Exact State}
\label{app:state}

\subsection{Eligible exposure and frontiers}

At a fixed routing state $t$, reuse the lineage-eligible set
$\Omega_t(s)=\{x:q_t(s,x)\text{ exists and }x\notin\mathcal B_s\}$ from
\Cref{sec:setting}.  The owner frontier is
\[
\F_t(x)=\argmax_{s:x\in\Omega_t(s)}q_t(s,x),
\]
with all exact ties retained and every frontier owner counted, regardless of
the frontier's raw score.  For each instance, define
\[
\mathcal C_t(x)=\{u:x\in\Omega_t(u)\},\quad N_x=|\mathcal C_t(x)|,\quad
y_{sx}=\ind\{s\in\F_t(x)\},\quad K_x=\sum_{u\in\mathcal C_t(x)}y_{ux}.
\]
When $N_x\ge2$, the per-observation credit is
\[
r_{sx}=y_{sx}-\frac{K_x-y_{sx}}{N_x-1}.
\]
The candidate aggregate is
\[
\Omega_t^{\mathrm{cmp}}(s)=\{x\in\Omega_t(s):N_x\ge2\},\quad
E_s^{\mathrm{cmp}}=|\Omega_t^{\mathrm{cmp}}(s)|,\quad
R_s=\sum_{x\in\Omega_t^{\mathrm{cmp}}(s)}r_{sx},\quad
G_s=R_s/E_s^{\mathrm{cmp}}.
\]
An $N_x=1$ observation remains committed but does not enter the aggregate.
Whenever a new finite observation changes $\mathcal C_t(x)$ or $\F_t(x)$, the
router recomputes $N_x$, $K_x$, and every affected candidate contribution on
that instance.  Candidates with $E_s^{\mathrm{cmp}}=0$ have undefined credit;
if every credit is undefined, routing and final selection fall back to the seed.

\subsection{Recursive proposal-lineage exclusion}

If parent $s$ is proposed on batch $P$, the prospective child cannot use its
current proposal instances or any ancestor birth-proposal instances as
admission evidence.  Let $P_a^{\mathrm{birth}}$ be the recorded proposal batch
that created accepted candidate $a$, with $P_0^{\mathrm{birth}}=\varnothing$
for the seed.  The excluded set is
\[
X(s,P)=P\cup\bigcup_{a\in\operatorname{Anc}(s)\cup\{s\}}
P_a^{\mathrm{birth}}.
\]
Admission batches are not themselves added to this birth provenance.  This
keeps proposal feedback recursively separate from the evidence that decides
whether the next child is admitted.  The lineage birth set $\mathcal B_s$
governs which committed observations may contribute to candidate $s$'s routing
key; $X(s,P)$ extends that exclusion to the prospective child's admission.

\subsection{Checkpoint state}

The optimizer checkpoint records the accepted programs, parent relation,
committed reward cache, frontier map, logical call count, active-queue cursor,
proposal assignments, sampled admission batches, and seeded random states.  The
queue cursor is interpreted modulo the current active order after every
recomputation.

\section{Algorithmic Details}
\label{app:algorithm}

\subsection{Proposal allocation implementation}

\Cref{eq:repairable_gap,eq:proposal_assignment} define the scientific
proposal objective.  The implementation builds the complete slot--batch
weight matrix from the committed cache and solves the resulting
maximum-weight assignment exactly.  Duplicate parent labels remain distinct
left-side slot vertices, and the operation never fills missing cache entries.

\subsection{Direct legal admission implementation}

For each of the $m\le w$ surviving proposal tasks, the owner directly samples
one batch $A_i$ from that task's admission sampler after removing every
instance in $X(s_i,P_i)$.  For each instance in the sampled batch, the owner
selects the defined-$G$ frontier owner with largest
$G$ as reference, using frozen randomness for an exact tie and the sampled
parent when no comparable owner exists.  The resulting plan is hidden from the
generator, required missing reference observations are filled on the existing
reference programs, and the fixed child is admitted exactly when the paired
aggregate in \Cref{eq:admission_gate} is positive.

\subsection{Wave execution}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{One \method{} wave}
\label{alg:compass_wave}
\footnotesize
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require committed routing state $\mathcal H_t$, wave width $w$, active cutoff $\rho$
\Ensure updated committed state $\mathcal H_{t+1}$
\State Recompute every defined $G_s$ from the committed cache
\State $\A_t \gets$ tie-inclusive Top-$\rho$ candidates, sorted by $G_s$ and candidate index
\State $z_{1:w} \gets \Call{CyclicSlots}{\A_t,w}$
\State Draw proposal minibatches $P_{1:w}$ and form $M_{ij}\gets H(z_i,P_j)$
\State $Y^\star \gets \Call{MaxWeightAssignment}{M}$
\ForAll{assigned parent--proposal pairs $(s_i,P_i)$ \textbf{in parallel}}
  \State Evaluate $s_i$ on $P_i$ and construct its reflection data
  \State Maximum-bound and commit the resulting parent observations
\EndFor
\State $\mathcal T\gets$ surviving proposal tasks; $m\gets|\mathcal T|\le w$
\ForAll{$(s_i,P_i)\in\mathcal T$ \textbf{in parallel}}
  \State $A_i\gets\Call{SampleAdmission}{s_i,P_i}$ subject to $A_i\cap X(s_i,P_i)=\varnothing$
  \State $r_i\gets\Call{BindReferences}{A_i}$; freeze and hide $(A_i,r_i)$ from the generator
  \State Evaluate and maximum-bound any missing reference observations
  \State $p_i\gets\Call{Reflect}{s_i,P_i}$; evaluate $p_i$ on $A_i$
  \State $\Delta_i\gets\sum_{x\in A_i}[q(p_i,x)-q(r_i(x),x)]$
  \If{$\Delta_i>0$}
    \State Commit $p_i$ and its lineage-eligible admission evidence
  \EndIf
\EndFor
\State Update the logical budget, frontiers, credits, and queue cursor; persist $\mathcal H_{t+1}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Repeat \Cref{alg:compass_wave} until the owner budget boundary.  Because the
logical budget is checked between waves, the final wave may overshoot; we report
both target and actual calls.  Request-level retries add no logical task result.

\section{Proofs for Evidence, Credit, and Regret}
\label{app:proofs}

The results below formalize three parts of the mechanism: recursive separation
creates a conditionally fresh paired admission channel, relative credit is
conserved within each comparable instance, and a canonical reference selector
exposes the fixed-budget pressure between search breadth and decision precision.

\subsection{Why generation and certification must separate}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \Cref{prop:self_cert_leakage}]
Let the task distribution be uniform on a domain of size $K\ge n/\epsilon$.
After observing $S=(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$, let the proposal rule construct a program
whose reward is one exactly on the distinct elements appearing in $S$ and zero
elsewhere.  Every reused certification example belongs to that set, so the
empirical reward on $S$ is one.  The population reward is the fraction of the
domain covered by the observed set, at most $n/K\le\epsilon$.
\end{proof}

The construction captures the mechanism in its simplest form: certification
is informative only when it contributes evidence beyond what already shaped
the candidate.  Recursive birth provenance extends this separation through the
lineage, because a descendant can retain behavior adapted to an ancestor's
proposal batch.

\subsection{Recursive exclusion creates a paired certification channel}

\begin{theorem}[Fresh paired admission]
\label{thm:recursive_fresh}
Let $\mathcal V$ be the information visible to the generator when child $p$ is
fixed from parent $s$ and proposal batch $P$, and suppose that $p$ is
$\mathcal V$-measurable.  Before generation, the owner binds an admission batch
$A=(X_1,\ldots,X_n)$ outside $X(s,P)$ and binds each reference $r(X_j)$ from
the committed pre-child state and frozen tie-breaking randomness.  Admission
identities, references, and outcomes are not visible in $\mathcal V$.  Then
\[
A\cap P=\varnothing,
\qquad
A\cap P_a^{\mathrm{birth}}=\varnothing
\quad\text{for every }a\in\operatorname{Anc}(s)\cup\{s\}.
\]
After $p$ is fixed, reveal the paired outcomes sequentially.  Let
$\mathcal H_{j-1}$ contain the fixed child, the owner binding state, and the
first $j-1$ revealed pairs, and define
\[
Z_j=q(p,X_j)-q(r(X_j),X_j),
\qquad
\mu_j=\E[Z_j\mid\mathcal H_{j-1}],
\]
\[
\widehat\mu_A(p;r)=\frac1n\sum_{j=1}^n Z_j,
\qquad
\overline\mu_A(p;r)=\frac1n\sum_{j=1}^n\mu_j.
\]
Because $Z_j\in[-1,1]$, for every $\delta\in(0,1)$, with probability at
least $1-\delta$,
\begin{equation}
\left|\widehat\mu_A(p;r)-\overline\mu_A(p;r)\right|
\le
\sqrt{\frac{2\log(2/\delta)}{n}}.
\label{eq:conditional_fresh}
\end{equation}
For the $i$th task, the implemented gate is equivalently
$\operatorname{admit}(p_i)\iff
\widehat\mu_{A_i}(p_i;r_i)>0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \Cref{thm:recursive_fresh}]
Because $X(s,P)$ contains both $P$ and every
$P_a^{\mathrm{birth}}$ for $a\in\operatorname{Anc}(s)\cup\{s\}$, legality
$A\cap X(s,P)=\varnothing$ gives the displayed disjointness relations.
After the child is fixed, each paired difference $Z_j$ lies in an interval of
length two.  Conditional Hoeffding--Azuma applied to
$Z_j-\E[Z_j\mid\mathcal H_{j-1}]$ gives
\[
\Pr\!\left(
\left|\widehat\mu_A(p;r)-\overline\mu_A(p;r)\right|\ge\epsilon
\right)
\le 2\exp\!\left(-\frac{n\epsilon^2}{2}\right).
\]
Setting the right-hand side to $\delta$ proves
\Cref{eq:conditional_fresh}.
\end{proof}

Equation~\eqref{eq:recursive_exclusion} supplies this separation through the
entire birth lineage.  COMPASS binds admission before child generation and
hides admission identities and references from the generator.  The resulting
paired contrast is therefore a conditionally fresh information channel for the
implemented owner gate.

\subsection{Relative credit is conserved instance-wise}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \Cref{prop:credit_conservation}]
For a fixed comparable instance $x$, use
$\sum_{s\in\mathcal C_t(x)}y_{sx}=K_x$ to obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{s\in\mathcal C_t(x)}r_{sx}
&=K_x-
\frac{\sum_{s\in\mathcal C_t(x)}(K_x-y_{sx})}{N_x-1}\\
&=K_x-\frac{N_xK_x-K_x}{N_x-1}=0.
\end{aligned}
\]
The statement is per instance; the candidate averages $G_s$ need not sum to
zero because their comparable exposure sets and denominators can differ.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The bandwidth--variance regret envelope}

To isolate the breadth--precision mechanism, consider a canonical
fresh-certification reference model.  Let $c_{\mathrm{prop}}$ denote the
rollout cost of producing one candidate before certification, and let $n\ge1$ be its fresh
admission support.  A budget $B$ can support
\[
M_B(n)=\left\lfloor\frac{B}{c_{\mathrm{prop}}+n}\right\rfloor
\]
distinct proposal-and-certification attempts.

For \Cref{prop:bandwidth_precision}, let $\mathcal F_{i-1}$ contain the
complete adaptive search history before candidate $p_i$ is certified and let
$p_i$ be measurable with respect to that history.  Conditional on
$\mathcal F_{i-1}$, its $n$ certification rewards are iid from the same owner
law and are hidden until $p_i$ is fixed.  The fresh-mean reference selector is
$\widetilde p_{M,n}\in\argmax_i\widehat R_n(p_i)$.

\begin{proof}[Proof of \Cref{prop:bandwidth_precision}]
Conditional on $\mathcal F_{i-1}$ and $p_i$, Hoeffding's inequality bounds
candidate $i$'s empirical error by $\epsilon$ with failure probability
$2e^{-2n\epsilon^2}$.  Taking expectations over the history preserves this
bound, and a union bound over $M$ candidates yields simultaneous error at most
$\epsilon=\sqrt{\log(2M/\delta)/(2n)}$.  Let $p^\star_M$ maximize population
utility among the generated candidates.  On this event,
$R(p^\star_M)-R(\widetilde p_{M,n})\le2\epsilon$.  Adding
$R^\star-R(p^\star_M)$ gives \Cref{eq:bandwidth_variance_regret}.
\end{proof}

Because rewards lie in $[0,1]$, taking expectations and bounding the complement
of the concentration event by one also gives
\[
\E\!\left[R^\star-R(\widetilde p_{M,n})\right]
\le
\E\!\left[R^\star-\max_{i\le M}R(p_i)\right]
+2\sqrt{\frac{\log(2M/\delta)}{2n}}+\delta.
\]
The two terms isolate the reference tradeoff.  Increasing $n$ tightens each
certification decision but lowers $M_B(n)$; increasing bandwidth raises the
chance of discovering a strong program but leaves less fresh support per
candidate.  Along a growing candidate sequence, the expected search-regret
term is nonincreasing in $M$, whereas the displayed selection term decreases
with $n$.  In COMPASS, recursive exclusion, instance-relative candidate
ranking, cyclic slots, and $H$-based proposal matching route the corresponding
budget across fresh decisions and new proposal attempts.

\subsection{Structural properties of the router}

\begin{proposition}[Cyclic slot coverage]
For a fixed ordered active set of size $m$, every block of $m$ consecutive
queue draws contains each active candidate exactly once, including when a
width-$w$ wave wraps around the queue.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof of cyclic slot coverage]
Index the fixed active order by $0,\ldots,m-1$ and let the persistent cursor
be $c$.  Consecutive draws are $(c+r)\bmod m$.  For
$r=0,\ldots,m-1$, these residues are pairwise distinct and cover every index.
Grouping the same sequence into blocks of width $w$ does not alter the argument,
including a block that wraps around the end of the queue.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Wave-optimal proposal matching]
Conditional on the $w$ drawn parent slots and $w$ proposal minibatches,
\Cref{eq:proposal_assignment} maximizes total recorded repairable gap over all
one-to-one slot--minibatch assignments.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof of wave-optimal proposal matching]
The $w$ parent slots and $w$ minibatches define a complete bipartite graph
with edge weight $H(z_i,P_j)$.  A feasible binary matrix in
\Cref{eq:proposal_assignment} is exactly a perfect matching, or equivalently a
permutation matrix.  Selecting the maximum-weight feasible matrix therefore
returns the maximum-weight matching.  Repeated parent labels do not change the graph because
their slot vertices remain distinct.
\end{proof}

\section{Detailed Experimental Protocol}
\label{app:protocol}

\subsection{Method identity}
\label{app:frozen_identities}

The method in \Cref{sec:method} uses
\texttt{leave\_one\_out\_frontier\_credit}, applies the tie-inclusive
Top-$\rho$ cutoff without ancestor masking, preserves the exact proposal
assignment in \Cref{eq:proposal_assignment}, and samples admission batches
directly per surviving proposal.  If the final selector returns seed index
zero, the protocol records a no-change
endpoint and skips official evaluation; otherwise, it evaluates the selected
artifact once.

\subsection{Owner evaluation integrity}

An endpoint enters a result table only when the optimization manifest is
terminal, the exact selected candidate and its source/config/model identities
are fixed, and the owner evaluation is complete.  IFBench requires 294 finite
example scores whose mean equals the aggregate.  AIME requires all 150 owner
problems.  HoVer requires 300 finite binary scores, and LiveBench-Math requires
all 126 frozen test positions under its owner aggregation.

Runs with an incorrect selected candidate, incompatible source identity, or
provider exhaustion converted to score zero are not eligible evidence.
Preflight results, intermediate checkpoints, and test-best oracle candidates
are never substituted for the selected owner artifact.

\subsection{Resource accounting}

We report logical task calls and final-wave overshoot separately from physical
language-model calls.  The latter include input/output tokens and route-priced
cost.  A local task deadline can produce an aligned failure observation; other
provider, schema, serialization, and engineering errors terminate the owner
batch.  A resumable run must preserve the exact source, config, cache,
checkpoint, and RNG identity.

\section{Broader Impact}
\label{app:impact}

Evidence routing can reduce the number of expensive environment executions
needed to discover a useful textual program and can make search decisions easier
to audit.  The same efficiency can also optimize undesirable procedures or
exploit imperfect rewards.  Strict admission and held-out tests do not replace
secure tool policies, sandboxing, red-team evaluation, or human review.

\paragraph{Use of large language models.}
Language models assisted with literature review, editing, code, and figure
design.  The authors verified every citation, algorithm, and artifact; model
output was neither experimental evidence nor used to choose test candidates.
